A 3.4 m hydraulic six-legged carrier walker: a paper concept study

A worked-through concept for a legged machine that carries 2 tonnes over rough ground, built from components that exist today. It has not been built and has not been tested. I am looking for technical review.

What this is

The machine has six legs around a central platform, a 1.7 m payload cube on top, two six-joint arms, electro-hydrostatic leg actuators and a hydrogen fuel cell power plant. The silhouette is borrowed from a fictional walker in a video game; everything inside is conventional engineering: steel tube legs, hydraulic cylinders, 70 MPa tanks, lidar and thermal cameras.

The study follows the usual path of a concept stage: requirements, size and proportions, layout and centre of mass, leg and actuator sizing, energy budget, power plant, electrical bus, control architecture and safety. Every figure is tagged as either a calculation from a script or a stated assumption, so the weak spots are visible.

Where it honestly stands

  • Most of the parts exist today at technology readiness 8 to 9: welded steel frames, hydraulic cylinders, forklift fuel cells, 70 MPa tanks, LiFePO4 batteries, lidar, thermal cameras.
  • Less mature: electro-hydrostatic actuators in walking machines (about 7), six-legged walking on real rough terrain (about 6), heavy machines working near people without a driver (about 5).
  • The whole machine sits at about 4. Nobody has built it.
  • Nothing here has been built or tested. A 40 cm bench-top scale model is planned as the first hardware step, followed by a single leg on a test stand.

What I am asking for

  • A critical technical review: which assumptions would you check first, and where is the concept most likely to fail?
  • Component data: datasheets or typical figures for cylinders, pumps and valves at these loads, or a pointer to the right product family.
  • Collaboration: a student project or lab exercise on any part (leg dynamics, gait, actuator sizing).

I am not selling anything. A short reply, even "your hip moment looks wrong", helps.
